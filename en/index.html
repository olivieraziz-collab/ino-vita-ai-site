§01 · The problem

The problem

The truth lives in five systems — and in nobody's head.

For a contractor with 5 to 100 employees, the real cost isn't the software. It's the coordination between the software.

Fragmented information

Email, drawings, job site, accounting: nothing talks to anything

The context behind every decision is scattered across the inbox, the drawings, the field tools and the books. Nobody has the full picture at the moment it matters.

The coordination tax

Your managers spend the day chasing and retyping

Collecting, following up, double-checking, re-keying information from one system to the next: low-value work with a high cost of error, eating the management capacity you actually pay for.

Risky automation

A generic AI assistant accelerates the mistakes too

Without cited sources, validation or sign-off, generic AI can speed up errors as fast as it speeds up work. In construction, a wrong amount or a missed directive is real money and real liability.

Control principles — built in, not bolted on

  • Read first: no action without cited sources

  • Human approval gates on every external or financial action

§07 · FAQ

Straight questions

The questions we actually get asked.

Q1Where does my data go? Who sees it?▸

Each client gets an isolated deployment: your data is never mixed with another client's. Agents work "read first" — they cite their sources —, every action lands in the audit trail, and an emergency kill switch cuts everything in one move.

These are the same rules we impose on our own construction operations, every day.

Q2Does this replace my current software?▸

No. The platform sits on top of your existing tools and does the work between them. At our company it orchestrates email, accounting, project management and document storage (Gmail, QuickBooks Online, Procore, Google Drive). Your tools remain the source of record; the agents do the coordination.

Q3What if the AI gets it wrong?▸

That's the question the system was designed around. Three locks, in order: (1) every significant deliverable goes through cross-validation by independent AI models, from different families than the author — the author never grades its own work; (2) nothing external or financial goes out without human approval; (3) everything is logged, so it's verifiable after the fact.

This mechanism has already caught real, documented errors on our side — before they reached a third party.

Q4Where does my team fit in?▸

At the center. The agents handle the collecting, preparing and following up; your people keep the decisions — and every client has one named, reachable person on our side: diagnostics by construction people, training, adoption follow-up, escalation to a human, always.

Q5How long before it's running at my company?▸

The rollout is gradual: diagnostic and baseline, then a guided pilot on a first workflow, then production. The timeline depends on your first workflow and your systems — it's set at the diagnostic. We don't promise a standard timeline before we know your process.

Q6Why should we believe you?▸

Because we're not asking you to believe us: the demo is our own system running on our own construction operations. And we won't publish any client-performance figure before it's been formally measured — metric, period, scope and source defined. What we show today are our internal facts, audited and dated.

§08 · About

About

Born on real job sites, not in a lab.

Groupe GIV / Ino-Vita is a Montreal construction group delivering institutional and commercial projects — schools, public buildings, multi-residential. In 2025–2026, the group hit the same wall as every mid-size contractor: coordination was devouring management capacity.

Instead of buying yet another piece of software, the team built a layer of specialized AI agents on top of its existing systems — governed by explicit rules: read first, sources cited, independent validation, human approval on anything external or financial, full logging, emergency stop.

That platform operates the company every day. The venture exists to industrialize it and offer it to contractors with 5 to 100 employees: AI agents that do the production work, surrounded by a small human team that sells, configures, trains — and stays reachable.
